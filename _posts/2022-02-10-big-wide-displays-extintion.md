This is a side story about how computer displays follow the TV mainstream.

## Curved TVs no more
Curved TVs were discontinued years ago, and big curved 16:9 computer screens went with them. TVs are watched from a distance, while curvature works best up close, so the industry was right to stop making curved TVs.

## Production lines split
Computer displays need curvature and TVs don't, so their technologies and manufacturing processes went separate ways.
Somehow entertainment won over productivity, and ultra-wide screens came to dominate big computer displays. Ultra-wide is better for cinema movies, games and video editing.

Without the TV industry behind them, displays like mine - [a 40-inch 16:9 Philips](https://www.philips.co.uk/c-p/BDM4037UW_00/brilliance-4k-ultra-hd-lcd-display-with-multiview) - can't be bought new anymore.
Today the biggest curved 16:9 display I've seen is a 32-inch Samsung.

## Why a big 16:9?
A better question is who it's for: anybody who works with code, text or websites. Text and websites need vertical space more than horizontal - websites especially, as the tall smartphone layout dominates the web.
A huge 16:9 is the most versatile: it works like two big, almost square screens side by side, or like a single TV. An ultra-wide only fits more windows, while a huge 16:9 can also give each window more content.

Thank God [3:2 laptops](https://www.theverge.com/2021/1/19/22238671/16-9-aspect-ratio-hp-elite-folio-dell-latitude-lenovo-thinkbook-plus-legion-7) are getting more popular, so at least one window on them has a proper height.

## Hail to the BDM4037UW

![My Philips BDM4037UW on the desk](https://user-images.githubusercontent.com/6271645/153392328-ea93b918-06c9-452a-9e3b-a6d9cca9bb93.png)

It's getting old and its response time is sluggish, but the blacks and colours are still great, and it's curved and huge (no scaling needed - like four 20-inch Full HD screens without bezels).
For work, movies, self-development and casual gaming it still has no competition, and used units often sell for more than they cost new.

I'd like to see a modern panel that is even bigger and more curved. I probably need to wait for 8K to become mainstream :(.
