Let's say you change smartphones quite often. Instead of throwing your old phone away, consider using it alongside your PC, laptop or tablet.

Attach your smartphone as a second screen.

There are many ways to attach the phone. For example, I reused clips for smartphone lenses. Patafix works too.

![A tablet clipped next to a laptop screen](https://tenonedesign.com/php/slir/w1100-q80/images/pd_mountie_guitar.jpg)

Imagine you have three spare smartphones, each attached hands-free next to your screen. On each of them you open an app you use on your main computer. For example, I use YouTube, Hangouts, Messenger, Facebook, Slack, Trello and Gmail. Above your screen you have a smartphone with a Hangouts video conference, on the left Facebook, on the right Trello with your notes. You can keep all of them open all the time without occupying your main screen (which usually shows the web browser).

PS: even with one smartphone you gain a lot. It can even be your only, everyday smartphone.

![A clip holding a smartphone on the side of a laptop lid](https://www.imore.com/sites/imore.com/files/styles/larger/public/field/image/2015/01/mountie-rear-shot-clasp.JPG?itok=CXmDNlDi)

You can use an additional screen in two ways:
- As an extended display for your main device - only the main computer's operating system is used.
- With a clipboard, keyboard and mouse shared between the host and the smartphone - here you use your smartphone's OS.

The first option is tricky. Without a cable, high-resolution streaming gets big lags. The software I found for this option was also too buggy to use. I've heard Apple has good solutions for that, but personally I only have an Android stack.

The second option doesn't require heavy data streaming. It's light enough to work effectively over any connection: Wi-Fi, Bluetooth ([Across Center](https://www.acrosscenter.com/)) or a USB cable ([DeskDock](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.floriandraschbacher.deskdock.pro&hl=en)).

![One keyboard and mouse shared between a computer, a laptop, a tablet and a phone](https://d1e6jkivd8jy0w.cloudfront.net/img/img_slide01.gif)

The most important part is the shared clipboard, which lets you easily integrate and combine all devices. If you find something interesting, you move to Trello on the right and save your finding. You can also share it on Facebook on the left. And you can do it discreetly while being on a video conference ;).

What you get:
- An ecological and pragmatic use of your old smartphones
- More productivity with stuff you already have
- Mobile screens you can take with your laptop
